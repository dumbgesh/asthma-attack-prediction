# Asthma Exacerbation — Biology & Science Notes

## 1. Asthma
Asthma is a chronic respiratory condition in which the airways become inflamed and narrowed, making breathing difficult.

## 2. Asthma Exacerbation
An asthma exacerbation is a worsening of asthma symptoms that can cause increased breathing difficulty and reduced lung function.

In this project, the target is:
- `Exacerbation`
- `No Exacerbation`

## 3. Asthma Control
Asthma control describes how well a person's asthma is being managed.

The dataset uses:
- ACT — Asthma Control Test
- ATAQ — Asthma Therapy Assessment Questionnaire

Poorer asthma control can be associated with greater exacerbation risk.

## 4. Lung Function

### FVC
Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) represents the amount of air that can be forcefully exhaled.

### FEV1
Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 second (FEV1) represents the amount of air exhaled during the first second of a forced breath.

### FEV1 Reversibility
Measures whether airflow improves significantly after bronchodilator medication.

## 5. Symptoms

The dataset includes:
- Daily symptoms
- Daily activity symptoms
- Nocturnal symptoms
- Dyspnea

These describe how asthma affects breathing, daily activities, and sleep.

## 6. Allergies & Clinical History

The dataset includes factors such as:
- Food allergies
- Drug allergies
- Allergic rhinitis
- Eczema
- Animal exposure
- Bronchiolitis
- Prenatal smoking exposure

These represent different aspects of the patient's allergic and respiratory history.

## 7. Other Patient Characteristics

The dataset also contains:
- Age category
- Gender
- BMI category
- Asthma category
- Breastfeeding history
- `Ath` — number/count related to the patient's asthma/allergen history as represented in the dataset

## 8. Important Finding from Our EDA

Some symptoms showed strong observed differences between exacerbation and non-exacerbation observations.

For example:
- Daily symptoms: 43.2% exacerbation rate vs 8.2% without daily symptoms.
- Daily activity symptoms: 51.5% vs 7.4%.
- Nocturnal symptoms: 66.7% vs 11.5%.
- Dyspnea: 75.0% vs 13.6%.

These are associations observed in this dataset, not proof that these factors independently cause exacerbations.

## 9. Key Idea

Asthma exacerbation is multifactorial.

It is influenced by a combination of symptoms, asthma control, lung function, allergies, demographics, and clinical history rather than one single variable.
